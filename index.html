Is the revenue miss a timing issue—or a permanent loss?

A six-series, six-quarter TV model connects delivery schedules, production costs, revenue, and cash. The analysis separates a $55M revenue timing shift from cost pressure and sizes the funding requirement.

ExcelPythonSQL
Read the case study

Approach. Build title-level forecasts, aggregate with SQL, and reconcile a timing → fee → cost variance bridge. Roll forward receivables, production assets, and participation payables to connect contribution to cash.

Decision. Treat delayed delivery as a liquidity planning issue while addressing production-cost increases separately. The FY26 contribution miss comprises $8.25M from timing and $9M from higher costs.

Scope. Fictional contracts and titles. Contribution is a project decision metric, not IFRS operating income. Full-horizon cost increases reduce contribution by $11M.

Positive lifetime profit. A different recovery story.

Connect changing revenue estimates to prospective amortization, current-period profit, and content-asset recovery.

Revised lifetime contribution$5.52M
Illustrative recovery-screen write-down$0.48M
Fictional title · revised scenario
Read the case study

Approach. Separate historical actuals, current results, and future revenue across four scenarios. Apply a prospective amortization ratio and test revenue haircuts against the remaining asset balance.

Decision. A positive lifetime contribution does not ensure recoverability of the remaining carrying amount. Revised future revenue of $38M produces a $0.48M warning in the simplified recovery screen.

Scope. Educational recovery screen only; it does not establish an IFRS impairment charge or an employer’s accounting treatment.
